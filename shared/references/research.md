# Research planning and synthesis

## Planning

Begin with the decision research will inform, uncertainty, and what finding would change the decision. Choose a method that can answer it:

| Question | Suitable approach | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Needs, motivations, current workflow | Interviews/contextual observation | Stated preference does not prove behavior |
| Can users complete this flow? | Moderated or unmoderated usability tasks | Task wording can bias behavior |
| How should information be grouped? | Card sorting/tree testing when tools permit | Proposed categories need validation |
| How widespread is an attitude? | Survey with an explicit sampling plan | Convenience samples are not population estimates |
| What happens over time? | Diary/longitudinal observation | Participation burden and missing records |
| Which variant changes an outcome? | Controlled experiment when feasible | Needs a defined metric and appropriate statistical design |

Source sample-size and timeline examples are planning hints, never universal thresholds. State recruitment criteria, relevant variation/access needs, consent/data handling, tasks/questions, collection method, analysis plan, and limitations proportional to the request. Do not contact participants or create an external study without authorization.

For interviews: establish context; ask neutral questions about actual experiences; probe concrete examples; distinguish concept reactions from observed behavior; finish with missing concerns. For usability: give a realistic goal without naming the control; record completion, detours, errors, assistance, and participant quotations. Avoid leading questions and success criteria retrofitted after seeing results.

## Synthesis

1. Identify records, participant IDs, dates, research method, and the actual denominator. Preserve source references; anonymize unnecessary personal details.
2. Extract observations and quotations without changing meaning. A quotation is the participant's verbatim words; a note-taker's paraphrase is reported as an observation. Never add attributes the records lack (gender, usage frequency, platform, intent), and keep conditional statements conditional. Keep interviewer inference separate.
3. Cluster related observations. Count people/records consistently with the denominator and participant IDs (for example 5 of 6: P1, P2, P4, P5, P6); one vocal participant is not multiple independent supporters.
4. Capture contradictions, counterexamples, and missing segments before naming themes.
5. Convert supported themes into implications/opportunities with evidence and confidence. Journey mapping and jobs-to-be-done are lenses, not new evidence.
6. Prioritize by user impact, evidence strength, reach, and effort. Show uncertainty instead of invented numerical precision.

Follow the research-synthesis section of the [quality bar](quality-bar.md). Deliver a concise executive account, evidence-backed themes, opportunities, limitations, and next questions. Use the [research report](../templates/research-report.md). Qualitative observations do not establish causation or population prevalence. Analytics may corroborate a pattern only if the metric and population actually match.

Optional integrations supply additional records, not assumed access. When only descriptions are supplied, produce a research plan or explicitly hypothetical design assumptions, not a fabricated synthesis.
